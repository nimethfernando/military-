USS LCS(L)(3)-102 Last of the WWII "Mighty Midgets"

Step aboard the "Yankee Dollar"—the last surviving World War II Landing Craft Support gunboat out of 130 built for the Pacific Theater. Measuring 158 feet and armed with more firepower per ton than a battleship, she fought at the Battle of Okinawa and stands today at Mare Island Naval Shipyard as a living memorial to the sailors who served aboard America's amphibious fire-support fleet.

Landing Craft Support Museum Corporation • 501(c)(3) Nonprofit

Preserving America's Last Surviving Amphibious Gunboat

Since her return from the Royal Thai Navy to the United States in 2007, our all-volunteer crew of U.S. Navy veterans and historians has worked tirelessly at Mare Island Naval Shipyard to restore the USS LCS(L)(3)-102 ("Yankee Dollar") back to her original 1945 World War II specifications.

Chartered with Six Primary Objectives, the Landing Craft Support Museum serves as both an active floating museum and the permanent national memorial to the men of LCS(L) 1–130:

1. Floating Museum Ship

Manage the operation, drydocking, and restoration of the former USS LCS(L)(3)-102 / HTMS Nakha as an authentic WWII museum vessel.

2. Sacred Naval Memorial

Perpetuate the memory of the officers and sailors of the U.S. Navy "Mighty Midgets" and Royal Thai Navy who gave their lives in service.

3. Relics & Wartime Archives

Acquire, preserve, and exhibit original deck logs, battle flags, artifacts, and mementos of the Landing Craft Support flotillas.

4. Pacific Theater Education

Inform the public of LCS accomplishments at Iwo Jima, Okinawa, the Philippines, Borneo, China, and the Occupation of Japan.

5. Youth Overnight Stays

Welcome Boy Scouts, Sea Cadets, and school groups aboard to experience life on a WWII naval vessel firsthand.

6. San Francisco Bay Heritage

Provide public dockside access at historic Mare Island and preserve naval maritime heritage around the San Francisco Bay Area.

You Don't Just Tour the Ship — You Step Into the Boots of Her 1945 Crew

Through the sights and sounds of steel decks, canvas-laced pipe berths, and ammunition handling rooms, visitors experience daily life aboard a 158-foot amphibious warship where 6 officers and 65 enlisted men lived, cooked in Galley A-111-L, gathered in Mess Hall A-208-L, and stood watch at Okinawa Radar Picket Station #18. Of the 130 LCS(L) gunboats built and roughly 10,000 sailors who served aboard them in WWII, the Yankee Dollar is the only intact ship remaining on earth.

From the Beaches of Okinawa to Mare Island

History of the "Mighty Midgets" & USS LCS-102

Developed after the assault on Tarawa demonstrated the deadly gap between capital-ship naval bombardment and troop landings, the Landing Craft Support (Large) packed battleship-grade close-in firepower onto a shallow-draft 158-foot steel hull.

1943 – FEB 1945

1. Birth of the "Yankee Dollar"

Designed by George Lawley & Sons on an LCI hull, 130 LCS(L) gunboats were built across three yards in Massachusetts and Portland, Oregon. Laid down Jan 13, 1945 at Commercial Iron Works in Portland and commissioned Feb 17, 1945 under Lt. Richard Jones, LCS-102 earned the nickname "Yankee Dollar" ($1.02).

APR – JUN 1945

2. Battle of Okinawa & Radar Picket #18

Rushed to the Pacific Theater, LCS-102 fought in the Battle of Okinawa (Gunto Operation, June 18–30, 1945). Assigned to Radar Picket Station #18 90 miles south of Okinawa, she screened fleet destroyers against relentless kamikaze attacks, earning her WWII Battle Star alongside heroic sister ships like LCS-86 and LCS-122.

JUL 1945 – APR 1946

3. Philippines & Occupation of Japan

Following Okinawa, LCS-102 conducted mine-removal operations at Leyte Gulf in the Philippines when WWII ended. She then sailed to Sasebo, Japan—arriving two months after the Nagasaki atomic bomb—where 40 crewmen toured the ruins of Nagasaki before serving occupation duty in China (Tientsin, Tsingtao) and Korea.

1946 – 1966

4. Reserve Fleet & JMSDF "Himawari"

Decommissioned in April 1946 at Astoria, Oregon and redesignated USS LSSL-102 in 1949, she was loaned to postwar Japan in 1953. Serving in the Japanese Maritime Self-Defense Force as JDS Himawari until 1966, she received her lookout deck roof and 81mm mortars still visible onboard today.

1966 – 2007

5. Royal Thai Navy & Voyage Home

Transferred to the Royal Thai Navy in 1966 as HTMS Nakha (LSSL-751), she served faithfully for 41 years. When the National Association of LCS(L) 1–130 discovered she was the last operational LCS in existence, veterans secured her transfer and transported her aboard a heavy-lift ship back to San Francisco Bay in 2007.

2007 – PRESENT

6. Mare Island Museum & Historic Register

Berthed at historic Mare Island Naval Shipyard in Vallejo, CA, the 102 has been lovingly restored by Navy veterans and volunteers led by Skipper Bill Mason and Executive Officer Gordon Stutrud. In October 2015, she was officially enshrined on the U.S. National Register of Historic Places.

Combat Doctrine & Pacific Theater Operations

Why the "Mighty Midgets" Looked Like Fourth of July Fireworks Leading an Assault

The Lesson of Tarawa (November 1943): After capital ships shelled invasion beaches to disrupt enemy defenses, naval bombardment had to lift as troop-laden landing craft approached the shore. In that critical window, defenders regrouped with deadly effect on U.S. Marines. Using the LCI(L) hull plans, George Lawley & Sons designed a purpose-built close-in fighting ship: the Landing Craft Support (Large).

Three Shipyards, 130 Ships: Lawley & Sons in Neponset, Massachusetts built 47 vessels; in Portland, Oregon, Albina Engine & Machine Works built 31 and Commercial Iron Works produced 52 (including the USS LCS-102).

Amphibious Assault Tactics: Attacking the beach in line abreast, LCS(L)s made two runs firing rocket barrages at 1,000, 800, and 500 yards before turning broadside to suppress targets of opportunity. On the third run, they slowed to let troop landing craft pass, then fired continuously over the heads of the Marines ashore—often carrying Marine or Army artillery spotters onboard.

Postwar Allied Legacy: Following minesweeping operations across Japan, Korea, China, and Taiwan, 53 LCS(L)s were loaned to Japan in the 1950s, while others served France, Italy, Greece, South Korea, Thailand, Taiwan, and the Philippines. Today, USS LCS-102 is the sole surviving vessel of all 130 built.

⚓ WHY FLOATING MUSEUM SHIPS REQUIRE CONTINUOUS SUPPORT

Unlike artifacts displayed inside climate-controlled buildings, the USS LCS(L)(3)-102 is a steel-hulled WWII warship berthed year-round in a corrosive marine environment. Restored by volunteers to a specific point in time—late summer 1945—every contribution goes directly toward fighting saltwater corrosion, maintaining authentic WWII equipment, and periodic drydock haul-outs.

Plan Your Visit to Mare Island Naval Shipyard

Our volunteer docents—many of them U.S. Navy veterans—proudly lead guided tours covering every level of the ship, from the twin diesel engine room to the pilothouse and gun decks.

⚓ Hours, Location & Guided Tours

📍 Waterfront Berth Address: Mare Island Naval Shipyard — Intersection of "A" Street & Nimitz Avenue (289 Waterfront Avenue), Mare Island / Vallejo, CA 94592.
🕒 Regular Visiting Hours: Tuesdays, Thursdays & Saturdays: 9:00 AM to 3:00 PM.
Note: Because the 102 is a working historic vessel that periodically enters drydock or undergoes deck repairs, please call ahead or check our Facebook page before traveling.
🎟️ Admission & Guided Access: Admission is 100% Free and dockside parking is free. Donations to help maintain the 102 are gratefully accepted aboard or online. Tour groups are guided in small groups (up to 6 visitors per guide) for safety on naval ladders and hatches.
🥾 Know Before You Board: 100% Original 1945 Configuration

As an authentic National Historic Landmark vessel, the Yankee Dollar has not had modern stairways cut into her hull. Visitors board via the gangway and navigate original WWII watertight doors, high coamings, and steep naval ladders. Please wear flat, closed-toe, non-skid shoes. Visitors unable to climb ladders can explore all 62 compartments via our 360° Virtual Museum.

⛺ "Nautical Nights" Youth & Scout Overnight Encampments

Boy Scout troops, Sea Cadets, and educational youth groups can arrange to spend the night or weekend sleeping in authentic WWII sailor bunks (Compartment A-208-1L) and dining on the Mess Deck (A-208-L) aboard the USS LCS-102. Contact Skipper Bill Mason (bmason6056@aol.com) or Exec. Officer Gordon Stutrud (707-373-2159) to schedule an overnight stay.
